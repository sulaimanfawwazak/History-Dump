## 1863-11-19

On November 19, 1863, during the American Civil War, President Abraham Lincoln delivered his momentous Gettysburg Address at the dedication of the Soldiers' National Cemetery in Gettysburg, Pennsylvania. This brief yet profound speech, delivered just four and a half months after the Union victory at the Battle of Gettysburg, redefined the purpose of the war. Lincoln invoked the principles of human equality enshrined in the Declaration of Independence and envisioned a 'new birth of freedom' for the nation. His words honored the fallen soldiers and articulated a vision for a unified nation dedicated to democratic ideals. The address cemented its place as one of the most famous speeches in American history.
