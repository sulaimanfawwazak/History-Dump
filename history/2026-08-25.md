## 1944-08-25

On August 25, 1944, during World War II, Paris was officially liberated from Nazi German occupation. After four years under German control, an uprising by the French Resistance, which began on August 19, prompted the rapid advance of Allied forces. The French 2nd Armored Division, under General Philippe Leclerc, and the U.S. 4th Infantry Division were instrumental in entering the city. German commander Dietrich von Choltitz surrendered to the Allies and the Resistance, defying Hitler's orders to destroy the city. The liberation was a significant strategic and symbolic victory, boosting Allied morale and hastening the end of the war in Europe.
