## 1883-08-27

On August 27, 1883, the volcanic island of Krakatoa, located between Java and Sumatra in the Dutch East Indies (modern-day Indonesia), experienced a series of four massive eruptions that culminated in one of the deadliest and most powerful volcanic events in recorded history. The final, most devastating explosion occurred at 10:02 AM local time, generating a colossal tsunami with waves up to 40 meters high that ravaged coastal towns and killed over 36,000 people. The sound of the eruption was heard nearly 3,000 miles away, making it the loudest sound in recorded history. Its ash plume reached 80 kilometers into the atmosphere, causing global climate effects and spectacular sunsets for years to come. The eruption obliterated most of the island and had a profound global impact on weather patterns and scientific understanding of volcanic power.
