## 1938-09-29

On September 29, 1938, the infamous Munich Agreement was signed in Munich, Germany, by the leaders of Nazi Germany, France, the United Kingdom, and Italy. This pact permitted Germany's annexation of the Sudetenland, a border region of Czechoslovakia predominantly inhabited by ethnic Germans. British Prime Minister Neville Chamberlain returned claiming "peace for our time," but the agreement is widely regarded as an act of appeasement that emboldened Adolf Hitler. Czechoslovakia was not invited to the conference and was forced to accept the terms, effectively sacrificing its territorial integrity in a vain attempt to avoid a larger European war.
