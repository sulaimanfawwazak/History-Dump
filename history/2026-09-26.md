## 1960-09-26

On September 26, 1960, the first-ever televised U.S. presidential debate between Democratic candidate Senator John F. Kennedy and Republican Vice President Richard Nixon was held in Chicago, Illinois. An estimated 70 million Americans watched, marking a transformative moment in political communication and media. Kennedy, appearing confident and composed on screen, was widely perceived by television viewers as having won the debate, contrasting with radio listeners who often thought Nixon performed better. This landmark event underscored the burgeoning influence of television in shaping public opinion. It fundamentally altered the dynamics of future American political campaigns, prioritizing visual presentation alongside policy substance.
