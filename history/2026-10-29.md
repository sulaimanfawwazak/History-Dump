## 1929-10-29

On October 29, 1929, the New York Stock Exchange experienced its most devastating collapse in history, known as "Black Tuesday." This catastrophic event saw the Dow Jones Industrial Average lose 12% of its value in a single day, following significant declines in the preceding week. Panic selling gripped investors, leading to billions of dollars in losses and wiping out fortunes overnight. While not the sole cause, Black Tuesday is widely considered a major catalyst for the Great Depression, initiating a decade of severe economic hardship in the United States and globally. The crash highlighted inherent instabilities in the financial system and prompted significant regulatory reforms in subsequent years.
