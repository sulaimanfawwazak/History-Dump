## 1967-08-30

On August 30, 1967, Thurgood Marshall was confirmed by the United States Senate to become an Associate Justice of the Supreme Court. Nominated by President Lyndon B. Johnson, Marshall's confirmation marked a pivotal moment in American history as he became the first African American to serve on the nation's highest court. Prior to this, he had a distinguished career as a civil rights lawyer, most notably arguing the landmark Brown v. Board of Education case before the Supreme Court. His appointment was a testament to his legal brilliance and his tireless fight for racial equality, profoundly influencing American jurisprudence for decades. Marshall served on the Supreme Court until his retirement in 1991.
