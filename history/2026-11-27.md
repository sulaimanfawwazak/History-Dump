## 1895-11-27

On November 27, 1895, Swedish chemist, engineer, and industrialist Alfred Nobel signed his final will and testament at the Swedish-Norwegian Club in Paris. In this pivotal document, he stipulated that the bulk of his considerable fortune, earned largely from his invention of dynamite, should be used to establish a series of prizes. These awards would annually recognize individuals who had conferred the greatest benefit to humankind in the fields of physics, chemistry, medicine, literature, and peace. This act laid the foundation for the prestigious Nobel Prizes, which have been awarded annually since 1901, profoundly impacting global scientific, literary, and peace efforts.
