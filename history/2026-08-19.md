## 1839-08-19

On August 19, 1839, French artist and physicist Louis Daguerre publicly presented his daguerreotype photographic process at a joint meeting of the French Academy of Sciences and the Académie des Beaux-Arts in Paris. This revolutionary process produced a highly detailed, permanent image on a silver-plated copper sheet. The French government had previously purchased the patent, immediately declaring it 'free to the world' and making the invention publicly available. This momentous announcement marked the official birth of practical photography, fundamentally transforming art, science, and the way people documented their lives and the world around them.
