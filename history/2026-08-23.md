## 1939-08-23

On August 23, 1939, just days before the outbreak of World War II, Nazi Germany and the Soviet Union signed the Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact in Moscow. This non-aggression treaty, named after the foreign ministers Joachim von Ribbentrop and Vyacheslav Molotov, publicly pledged that neither country would attack the other. Secretly, however, the pact contained protocols that divided Eastern Europe into German and Soviet spheres of influence, including the partition of Poland, Finland, Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania, and Bessarabia. This agreement cleared the way for Germany's invasion of Poland on September 1, 1939, initiating the war, and had profound implications for the political landscape of Europe.
