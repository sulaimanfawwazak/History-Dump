## 1893-09-19

On September 19, 1893, New Zealand achieved a landmark moment in democratic history by becoming the first self-governing nation to grant all women the right to vote in parliamentary elections. This significant milestone was the culmination of years of tireless campaigning by suffragists, most notably Kate Sheppard, who led the Women's Christian Temperance Union's efforts. Governor Lord Glasgow signed the new Electoral Bill into law, making New Zealand a pioneering example for women's suffrage movements worldwide. The newly enfranchised women participated in the general election held in November of that year, demonstrating the immediate impact of this progressive legislation.
