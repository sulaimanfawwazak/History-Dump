## 1981-08-12

On August 12, 1981, International Business Machines (IBM) officially unveiled its Personal Computer, model 5150, in New York City. This groundbreaking machine, often simply called the IBM PC, was developed by a team of twelve engineers and designers led by Don Estridge in Boca Raton, Florida. It featured an open architecture and used third-party components and software, including an Intel 8088 microprocessor and Microsoft's PC-DOS operating system. The release of the IBM PC was a pivotal moment in computing history, standardizing many aspects of personal computers and igniting the rapid growth of the PC market. Its success led to a vast ecosystem of compatible hardware and software, profoundly impacting businesses and homes worldwide.
